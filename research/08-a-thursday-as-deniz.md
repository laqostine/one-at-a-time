# A Thursday, as Deniz

*Persona exercise. Deniz, 28, hard of hearing since 19, aids in both ears, lipreads, designer on a marketing team of six, lives with hearing parents and a younger brother. Written after reading `reddit/threads.md`, `reddit/wishlist.md` and `04-final-synthesis.md`.*

Ratings are 1-5. **Dignity**: 5 means I feel like a person, 1 like a patient. **Effort**: how much the hearing people have to do (5 = a lot). **Demo**: how easy it is to show in 90 seconds tonight with a laptop and three phones.

---

## 07:40 Breakfast

**What I lose:** Mum tells me from the stove, with her back to me, that Aunt Nesrin is coming to dinner. Emir says something with his mouth full. I hear the kettle perfectly. I don't hear either of them.
**How I cope today:** "What?" twice, and then Mum says "never mind, later." Later means I walk in tonight to a guest I didn't know was coming.
**How it feels:** Like a lodger. I'm loved, but I'm a lodger.

**Solution: Fridge Line** *(no AI)*
Anything that changes the day has to go on the fridge whiteboard before anyone leaves the kitchen. One line, one marker, and it hangs on a string so nobody can say they couldn't find a pen. It isn't a note for Deniz. It's the family's to-do list, and I just happen to need it most.
*Who changes:* them. *Embarrassing?* No, Dad already uses it for the gas bill. **Dignity 5 · Effort 2 · Demo 1**

## 08:30 Commute

**What I lose:** The metro stops between stations and the driver announces something. Everyone else sighs. I sit there not knowing whether we're waiting two minutes or twenty.
**How I cope today:** I read the faces around me. If people start getting off, I get off.
**How it feels:** Like a dog watching the humans for cues.

**Solution: Tap-Me Pin** *(no AI)*
A small enamel pin on my bag, well designed (I'm allowed to be vain), with an ear and a hand. It says "tap my shoulder, then talk." It turns strangers from shouting at the back of my head into one tap and a face.
*Who changes:* strangers, and me, because I'd have to wear it. *Embarrassing?* Some days, yes. **Dignity 3 · Effort 1 · Demo 2**

## 09:30 Standup

**What I lose:** Six of us stand in a loose circle and nobody goes in any order. By the time I've found who's talking, I've missed the first four words, and the first four words are the ones that matter ("Deniz, can you…"). One Reddit comment says "whiplash," and that's exactly it.
**How I cope today:** I stand next to Selin because she talks slowly. I nod. I DM her afterwards: "did anyone ask me something?"
**How it feels:** Nodding along is lying all day in small ways.

**Solution: The Talking Mug** *(no AI)*
There's one ugly team mug, and whoever is holding it has the floor. You pass it on to the next person, you don't toss it. So I know where to look *before* anyone opens their mouth. The mug gives my eyes a head start.
*Who changes:* them, a little. *Embarrassing?* No. It's a team ritual, and everyone ends up loving the ugly mug. **Dignity 5 · Effort 2 · Demo 3**

## 10:50 The hallway joke

**What I lose:** Four people at the coffee machine all laugh at once. I show up for the laugh and miss the joke.
**How I cope today:** I fake-laugh. Or I ask, and then it has to be told again to a silent audience, which kills it, and I'm the one who killed it.
**How it feels:** Humour is how people bond. I've been locked out of the bonding and left with the noise.

**Solution: Punchline Card** *(app)*
The room phone is already listening as part of our table ledger. When it hears a laughter spike, it pins the last ~12 seconds as a card on my phone headed "the room laughed at:". I get the joke five seconds late, quietly, and I can laugh for real, without anyone having to perform it again.
*Who changes:* nobody. *Embarrassing?* No, it's private. **Dignity 4 · Effort 1 · Demo 4**

## 12:40 Lunch

**What I lose:** The waiter asks "still or sparkling, fries or salad" into the noise. The team sits me facing the window, so every face is a silhouette. The conversation about the Q4 offsite happens in my blind spot.
**How I cope today:** I order whatever Selin orders. I look at my phone and people assume I'm aloof.
**How it feels:** Hungry for the wrong thing, in every sense.

**Solution: Window-at-My-Back** *(no AI)*
Team rule: Deniz sits with her back to the light, so faces are lit and hers is the one in shadow. Orders go on the paper slip the restaurant already has, passed round the table, one handwriting per line. It costs no money and no app, and it doubles what I can lipread.
*Who changes:* them, once, and then it becomes habit. *Embarrassing?* Only the first time I explain it. **Dignity 5 · Effort 1 · Demo 1**

## 14:00 The 1:1

**What I lose:** Burak, my manager, sits against the glass wall with the afternoon sun behind him and types while he talks. Somewhere in there he says the word "visibility" about my performance. I don't know if it was praise or a warning.
**How I cope today:** I ask him to repeat it. He says it differently, so now I have two half-sentences. I nod, then go and cry a bit in the stairwell.
**How it feels:** The conversations that matter most are the ones where I'm most afraid to say "what?"

**Solution: Face Lamp + Three Lines** *(no AI)*
The 1:1 room gets a warm clip lamp aimed at the chair the manager sits in, and his laptop lid is closed while he talks. At the end he writes three lines on a card: what we decided, what I'm doing, what he's doing. I keep the card, and I have something to hold onto besides my memory of his lips.
*Who changes:* him. *Embarrassing?* No. Honestly, every employee should get the card. **Dignity 5 · Effort 3 · Demo 2**

## 15:30 Afternoon sync

**What I lose:** I turn to the screen to show my mockups. Can starts talking behind me, and nobody notices that I've stopped hearing.
**How I cope today:** I swivel back and forth like a sprinkler and miss most of the design feedback on my own work.
**How it feels:** Invisible during the one moment I'm supposed to be the centre.

**Solution: Behind-You Buzz** *(app)*
Each phone on the table is a named mic. My laptop camera notices when I'm facing away from the room. If a named phone starts talking while I'm turned, my phone buzzes "Can, behind you, left" and keeps the few seconds I missed so I can catch up.
*Who changes:* nobody. *Embarrassing?* No, just a buzz. **Dignity 4 · Effort 1 · Demo 5**

**What else I lose here:** I have a point, but the gap closes before I can get it out. By the time I speak, they've moved on and I sound like I wasn't listening.
**Solution: Next-Gap Line** *(app)*
I type my point and the app says it out loud at the next real pause, under my name. The hearing people get a pace bar on their phones that turns amber when turns are coming too fast. My point lands while it's still relevant.
*Who changes:* them, a bit (the bar is a nudge). *Embarrassing?* The synthetic voice makes me wince. **Dignity 3 · Effort 2 · Demo 5**

## 18:10 The group chat

**What I lose:** "LOL the Brian thing 😂😂" and eight reactions. The Brian thing happened out loud at 16:45. Even the written channel, the one place I'm supposed to be equal, points back to talk I never had access to.
**How I cope today:** I react with 😂 too, which is lying with an emoji.
**How it feels:** Like a second exclusion, and it follows me home on my phone.

**Solution: Footnote Rule** *(no AI)*
If you mention something that was said out loud, you add one line of context in brackets: "(Brian put the client's logo upside down on the deck)." It's a team norm, so it isn't done for Deniz. It helps the person who was on leave and the new intern too.
*Who changes:* them. *Embarrassing?* No. **Dignity 5 · Effort 2 · Demo 2**

## 19:30 Family dinner

**What I lose:** Aunt Nesrin is here (surprise). Four people, three conversations going at once, and Dad talking to Emir sideways. This is Dinner Table Syndrome, and everyone on r/deaf has it.
**How I cope today:** I smile and eat. At 20:15 I say I'm tired, and I am, from lipreading.
**How it feels:** Loved, and not included in anything.

**Solution: Crosstalk Lamp** *(app)*
A phone stands on a placemat in the middle of the table, and every family phone becomes part of the same room. When two voices overlap, all the phones glow amber. When one person holds the floor, they go calm green. It needs no transcription at all. It's a mirror for the hearing people, so it's never me having to say "one at a time" for the hundredth time.
*Who changes:* them, and they can actually see it. *Embarrassing?* No. The lamp is the one saying it, not me. **Dignity 5 · Effort 2 · Demo 5**

## 22:15 The doorbell, shower, aids out

**What I lose:** A neighbour drops off a parcel. Emir has headphones on. It's a "missed delivery" note on the door, for the third time this month.
**How I cope today:** I shower at weird times and refresh the tracking link.
**How it feels:** The only time I'm truly off duty is also when the house forgets I'm deaf.

**Solution: Aids-Out Hook** *(no AI)*
There's a hook outside the bathroom door. When my aids hang on it, the door is officially somebody else's job. You can see it from the hallway without asking. Add a £12 flashing bulb wired to the bell and I'll know that someone came.
*Who changes:* them, and it's triggered by an object, not a nag. *Embarrassing?* No. **Dignity 5 · Effort 2 · Demo 1**

*Tally: 7 no-AI (Fridge Line, Tap-Me Pin, Talking Mug, Window-at-My-Back, Face Lamp + Three Lines, Footnote Rule, Aids-Out Hook), 4 app (Punchline Card, Behind-You Buzz, Next-Gap Line, Crosstalk Lamp).*

---

## Stepping out: the three picks, and the one warning

**1. Crosstalk Lamp (placemat + three phones as one room).** Judges have seen captions a hundred times. They have never seen an accessibility tool whose screen faces the *hearing* people. It needs no transcription, just overlap detection across named phones, and it turns "one at a time, please" from the deaf person's hundredth request into ambient light. **30-second moment:** three phones stand on a placemat. Two of us deliberately talk over each other, and all three phones go amber together. One person stops, and they all go green. Deniz hasn't said a word, and the room has fixed itself.

**2. Behind-You Buzz (laptop camera + named phones).** This covers the gap every captioning product ignores: the moment you're not looking. The camera catches Deniz turning to the screen, a named phone detects speech, and her phone buzzes with *who* and *where*, holding the seconds she missed. **30-second moment:** Deniz turns to point at a slide. "Can" speaks from a phone behind her. Her phone buzzes "Can, behind you, left", she turns back, and the missed line is waiting as a card. It's cause and effect in one gesture, which is exactly what a judge remembers.

**3. The Talking Mug × Punchline Card (a physical token for the social layer, the app for the leftovers).** Present it as a pair: the mug is a zero-tech ritual that fixes turn-taking, and the Punchline Card handles what no ritual can catch, the laugh. **30-second moment:** hold up the ugly mug and pass it, and the ledger labels the new speaker. Then someone tells a one-liner, two people laugh, and Deniz's phone shows "the room laughed at:" with the line. She laughs, late but for real. The pitch line is "we don't just caption the meeting, we give her the joke."

**Most likely to be wrong or offensive: Next-Gap Line.** Having a synthetic voice speak *for* a deaf person, and letting software decide when there's a "gap" she's allowed to speak into, puts the machine in charge of her voice and her timing. To culturally Deaf people, many of whom choose not to voice, being "voiced" by a device reads as making them pass as hearing. It also assumes that the fix is making her fit spoken turn-taking, not asking the hearing room to slow down. If we keep it, it has to be opt-in, her own recorded voice or visible text only, and never the default. Runner-up is the Tap-Me Pin, which puts the label, and the burden, on the deaf person in public.
