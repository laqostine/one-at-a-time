Even when verbatim captions or live Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) 
transcriptions are available, deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) individuals miss 
critical non-verbal, spatial, emotional, and environmental cues during live 
conversations and meetings [1, 2]:

* **Speaker Identity, Direction of Address, and Spatial Localization:** 
  * Standard live captions frequently omit speaker tags or fail to distinguish 
between multiple speakers, creating severe context fragmentation and confusion 
in group discussions [3-5].
  * Physical group settings strip away spatial sound localization; while hearing
peers instantly locate a speaker using binaural audio, DHH participants must 
visually scan the room, often missing the first several seconds of an utterance 
[5].

* **Vocal Tone, Emotional Nuance, and Affective Prosody:** 
  * Live captions deliver speech as flat, unformatted text, stripping away 
prosody—such as pitch inflection, vocal stress, cadence, and loudness—that 
carries emotional intent [1, 6, 7].
  * Sarcasm, urgency, tentativeness, or irony are lost in verbatim text (e.g., a
sarcastic comment transcribed as "That was a fantastic idea" is interpreted 
literally unless non-verbal cues are explicit) [7, 8].
  * Neuroimaging (EEG) demonstrates that standard captions activate voluntary 
attentional and language-processing brain circuits rather than triggering the 
immediate, automatic emotional processing elicited by acoustic sound [9-11].

* **Environmental Sounds and Non-Speech Information (NSI):** 
  * Non-Speech Information—such as laughter, applause, doors opening, fire 
alarms, or ambient room noise—is systematically omitted by speech-focused ASR 
pipelines [12, 13]. 
  * Automated ASR focuses exclusively on primary speech tokens, excluding DHH 
users from shared room atmosphere and situational sound cues [8, 13].

* **Side Comments, Live Objections, and Interjections:** 
  * Off-microphone side comments, background chatter, and quick interjections 
are frequently filtered out or dropped by live ASR engines [13].
  * Post-meeting AI summaries record only final conclusions, completely erasing 
intermediate live objections, debates, or unstated nuances that occurred while a
decision was actively forming [14].

* **Conversational Turn-Taking and Social Footing:** 
  * Conversational turn-taking relies on subtle acoustic cues like trailing 
pitch, breath intake, or micro-pauses [8, 15].
  * Caption processing latency (from 300 ms up to several seconds) delays the 
DHH user's awareness of conversation flow, causing them to accidentally 
interrupt others or make interjections that are already obsolete [8, 15, 16].

* **Caption Latency and Text Instability:** 
  * Broadcast TV captions carry delays of 7–12 seconds, while typical ASR 
captions carry a 1–2 second delay [16, 17].
  * Latency creates a frustrating disconnect with visual reactions (seeing 
people laugh or react before the corresponding text appears) [17-19].
  * Raw ASR causes word flickering and dynamic recalculations on screen; the 
visual system registers these updates as motion events, drawing peripheral 
attention away and disrupting reading trajectories [20].

* **Eye-Contact Trade-offs and Visual Split-Attention:** 
  * DHH users face a mandatory **visual split-attention constraint**, constantly
shifting gaze between the speaker's face/mouth, presentation slides, physical 
equipment, and the caption stream [21-23].
  * Looking down at scrolling captions forces users to break eye contact, 
depriving them of visual speech cues (lip movements, facial expressions, hand 
gestures) [7, 21, 23]. Crucial visual information is systematically lost during 
the saccadic eye movements required to switch visual focus [23, 24].

* **Elevated Cognitive Workload and Working Memory Fatigue:** 
  * Forcing all instructional, social, and linguistic information through the 
visual channel imposes a high extraneous cognitive load, requiring sustained 
voluntary mental energy [11, 23, 25, 26].
  * Unpredictable live speaker pacing prevents metacognitive control over 
reading speed (preventing pausing or scanning back), forcing continuous 
cognitive hyper-vigilance and leading to severe mental fatigue and 
teleconference exhaustion [26-28].
